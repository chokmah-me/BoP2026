# Background: The Origin of Balance of Power 2026

## From Amiga 1000 to Modern Multipolar Simulation

**Balance of Power 2026** was born from a childhood memory that never left me.

During the final years of the Cold War, I spent countless hours on my **Amiga 1000** playing **Balance of Power** (1985) by Chris Crawford. That game — with its elegant simulation of US-Soviet relations, crisis management, and the ever-present shadow of nuclear escalation — left a profound impression on me.

Even as a child, I was captivated by how a relatively simple computer game could capture the tension, uncertainty, and strategic depth of great-power competition. The way small decisions could cascade into global consequences felt both thrilling and terrifying. It was one of the first times I truly understood that international relations was not just about tanks and missiles — it was about perception, miscalculation, patience, and risk.

Decades later, as the world once again entered a period of multipolar tension — with great-power competition returning to the forefront in ways not seen since the 1980s — I found myself thinking about that old Amiga game. The world had changed dramatically, but the fundamental dynamics of crisis, escalation, and strategic choice remained eerily familiar.

## Why BoP2026 Exists

**Balance of Power 2026** is my attempt to bring that spirit of strategic simulation into the 21st century.

While the original *Balance of Power* focused on the bipolar US-Soviet rivalry, today’s world is far more complex. We face:

- Multiple great powers (US, China, Russia, India, EU, Gulf states, Iran)
- Six domains of competition (military, economic, cyber, information, diplomatic, domestic)
- Rapid technological change (AI, autonomous systems, space, biological risks)
- The constant danger of compound crises and strategic surprise

BoP2026 was designed as both:

1. A **pedagogical tool** for teaching international relations and war studies
2. A **research companion** for exploring counterfactuals and parameter sensitivity in multipolar crises

It is a direct spiritual successor to that formative Amiga experience — updated for a new era of great-power competition.
